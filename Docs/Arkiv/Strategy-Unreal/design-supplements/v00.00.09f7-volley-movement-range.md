# PROJECT 1864 – v00.00.09f7 Volley, Movement and Range Design Supplement

Status: TEST design baseline for the 1851 company-scale tactical prototype.

## Purpose

This supplement defines the first readable soldier-state animation pass, compact tactical musket ranges, formation-accurate tactical markers, casualty placement and differentiated movement speeds.

## Fire range and facing

The current tactical TEST scale remains **1 Unity world unit = 1 metre**.

Current 09f7 gameplay values:

- Close: **40 m**
- Medium / Effective: **80 m**
- Long / Maximum: **115 m**
- Fire sector: **70 degrees total**, +/-35 degrees from unit facing

These values are deliberately compact for the current prototype map and are not the final historical weapon database. Final ranges, accuracy curves and ammunition behaviour shall be defined per weapon and doctrine later.

The visual cone and actual permitted fire direction should remain aligned. Facing therefore matters tactically: an infantry company should not effectively engage a target far outside its frontal sector without turning.

## Soldier fire-state cycle

Infantry uses the following readable states:

1. **AWAIT** – weapon held ready while waiting for the next firing cycle.
2. **AIM** – rifle/musket is brought into the firing position.
3. **FIRE** – short recoil/shot pose and volley smoke emission.
4. **RELOAD** – weapon returns to a loading position.
5. Back to **AWAIT**.

Later animation work may subdivide reload into historically appropriate motions such as cartridge handling, ramrod use, priming and return-to-ready. The present states are intended to make tactical behaviour readable at RTS camera distance.

## Volley smoke

A formed volley must read visually as a **company-front event**, not as isolated smoke from a few arbitrary soldiers.

09f7 therefore emits black-powder smoke across the full firing frontage. Individual particles vary in position and lifetime so the smoke does not appear as a solid wall, but the complete line should visibly contribute to the volley.

Smoke is world-space after emission. Old smoke remains over the ground while troops advance through or away from it.

Future work can add wind, humidity, accumulated battlefield haze and visibility penalties.

## Movement animation states

Movement must not look like soldier models sliding across terrain.

09f7 introduces:

- **WALK** – normal ordered movement.
- **FORCED MARCH** – visibly faster, more energetic marching.
- **RUN / ROUT** – routed or panicked soldiers flee at a higher speed.

Current TEST speeds:

| Movement | Denmark | Prussia |
|---|---:|---:|
| Normal walk | 3.20 m/s | 3.35 m/s |
| Forced march | 4.25 m/s | 4.45 m/s |
| Rout / panic run | 5.20 m/s | 5.40 m/s |

These are prototype gameplay speeds rather than final march-doctrine values.

## Forced March

Forced March is a player-controlled movement mode, currently toggled with **G** / `TVANG G`.

Design intent:

- increases tactical movement speed;
- is best used to reposition before contact;
- imposes additional cohesion/fatigue cost while moving;
- should become increasingly unattractive close to combat;
- does not replace the automatic Line -> Column march-formation logic;
- is cancelled by rout/panic, because routed units use the RUN state instead.

Current extra cost is **0.22 cohesion points per second** in addition to the normal movement cohesion cost.

Future versions should migrate this into a broader fatigue/stamina system with recovery, weather, terrain, load, training and officer effects.

## Line and march column

The 1851 Danish line baseline remains three ranks. Long movement continues to reform the company into a marching Column and deploy it back into Line near the final position/contact.

Selection and destination markers must follow the actual formation slots rather than a fixed abstract rectangle.

This is especially important for Column because the current column extends primarily behind the regiment pivot. The marker centre therefore uses the actual min/max formation bounds, not the pivot itself.

## Casualties

A casualty should visually fall where the formation actually stood when the loss occurred.

09f7 rules:

- select a real formation slot at the moment the casualty is recorded;
- place the fallen visual at that world-space slot;
- allow only approximately +/-0.30 m local variation;
- leave the fallen visual in world space when survivors move away.

Fallen soldiers must not appear several metres away from the formation simply to create a wider decorative casualty field.

## Future animation extensions

Candidate later states include:

- Present / ready weapon
- Kneel
- Bayonet / melee ready
- Melee attack and defence
- Hit reaction
- Wounded / incapacitated
- Surrender
- Rally after rout
- Exhausted forced-march movement
- Officer commands and signalling
- Drummer / bugler animation

The underlying principle is that tactical state should be readable from soldier behaviour wherever practical rather than only from UI text.
